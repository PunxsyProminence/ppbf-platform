# SHADOW Canonical Authority Model

Status: Canonical doctrine and architecture source of truth for Punxsy Prominence.

Scope of this document:

- Defines SHADOW as the adaptive organizational intelligence system.
- Sets doctrine and authority model for future backend, frontend, AI, telemetry, video, sensor, and multi-gym work.
- Provides evidence-based alignment audit of the current repository.
- Does not change runtime behavior.

## 1) SHADOW Canonical Definition

SHADOW stands for Systemic Holistic Analytics and Diagnostic Oversight Wing.

SHADOW is the adaptive organizational intelligence system of Punxsy Prominence.

SHADOW is not a single feature. SHADOW is not only a chatbot, dashboard, telemetry stream, upload flow, athlete tracker, admin module, video analyzer, or AI utility.

SHADOW uses those capabilities as parts of a larger organizational learning system.

## 2) Symbolic Meaning

The SHADOW identity is intentionally multi-layered:

1. Military operations context:
- observation
- intelligence
- mission tracking
- after-action learning
- chain-of-command boundaries
- operational decision loops

2. Boxing context:
- shadow boxing
- movement rehearsal
- skill refinement
- invisible preparation before visible performance

3. Punxsutawney and Groundhog context:
- shadow as local cultural symbol
- prediction, observation, adaptation, seasonal awareness

4. Organizational growth context:
- as the organization grows, SHADOW expands
- each gym, athlete, coach, decision, upload, failure, success, and discovery increases SHADOW value

SHADOW grows as Punxsy Prominence grows.

## 3) Primary Mission

SHADOW has two permanently linked missions:

1. Help users perform their roles better.
2. Continuously gather, validate, and learn from evidence to improve future performance.

Everything else supports these missions.

The boxing gym is the proving ground where SHADOW learns, validates, and matures.

Boxing provides:

- engagement

## 3C) Recursive Observation Doctrine

Most systems are linear:

Observe -> Decide -> Act -> Result

SHADOW is recursive.

Every:

- observation
- decision
- action
- success
- failure
- correction
- discovery

can generate a new observation.

Examples:

Decision -> creates observation
Failure -> creates observation
Success -> creates observation
Correction -> creates observation
Discovery -> creates observation

The chain does not end; each node can begin another chain.

## 3D) Knowledge Web Doctrine

SHADOW does not build expertise through one loop.
SHADOW builds expertise through interconnected loops.

Examples:

Observation -> Decision -> Action -> Success -> Observation
Observation -> Failure -> Observation
Observation -> Discovery -> Observation
Observation -> Correction -> Observation

Over time:

Many observation loops -> Knowledge Network -> Experience Graph -> Expertise -> Institutional Knowledge

This expanding network is the SHADOW Knowledge Web.

Capability growth must expand the SHADOW Library.

If the organization adds a new capability, route, workflow, role surface, evidence source, or decision pattern, SHADOW must grow its library to match.

That growth may include:

- new observations
- new evidence classes
- new educational materials
- new research requirements
- new validated lessons
- new doctrine candidates
- new organizational memory artifacts

No meaningful capability should exist without increasing SHADOW's learning surface.

## 3E) Organizational Memory Rule

Every:

- success
- failure
- correction
- discovery
- research finding
- coaching insight
- administrative lesson
- governance lesson

must become recoverable organizational knowledge.

SHADOW preserves institutional memory.

## 3O) Master and Scoped SHADOW Model

SHADOW is one system with many scoped expressions.

- doctrine
- evidence gaps
- research requirements
- unresolved conflicts
- cross-subject patterns
- capability growth
- library growth
- organizational learning

Master SHADOW does not replace governance, providers, or human authority.

Below Master SHADOW are scoped SHADOW views.

Examples:

- Athlete SHADOW
- Coach SHADOW

- Research SHADOW
- Safety SHADOW

Scoped SHADOW views inherit doctrine from Master SHADOW.

Scoped SHADOW views do not create independent truth.

Examples:

- Billy's SHADOW

These are scoped learning and evidence views, not sovereign intelligences.

Subject SHADOW may:

- organize observations
- track subject-specific patterns

- override Master SHADOW doctrine
- create policy automatically
- diagnose
- clear participation
- replace authorized humans

If a Subject SHADOW conflicts with doctrine or lacks strong evidence, it must create a research requirement or review event.

## 4) Three-Engine Model

### Engine 1: Human Development Engine

Purpose: Improve role performance through guidance, education, accountability, and decision support.

Role examples:

- Athletes: goals, readiness, training guidance, skill development, session feedback.
- Coaches: planning, athlete management, reviews, technical development, teaching improvement.
- Parents and Guardians: progress understanding, support guidance, boundary clarity, communication quality.
- Administrators: operations workflows, compliance awareness, documentation quality.
- Board Members: governance visibility, organizational health, strategic awareness.
- Partner Gyms: onboarding, shared learning, program improvement.

### Engine 2: Intelligence Acquisition Engine

Purpose: Gather evidence from all relevant sources and preserve institutional knowledge.

Input sources include:

- user interactions
- athlete records
- goals
- sessions
- coach reviews
- attendance
- readiness
- operational records
- admin records
- uploads and documents
- research
- partner gym contributions
- historical records
- feedback
- outcomes, failures, and successes

Outcomes:

- identify what works and fails
- identify why
- improve future training, coaching, operations, governance, and safety

### Engine 3: Observational Analysis Engine

Purpose: Learn from observational inputs that humans may miss.

Future observational inputs:

- video node detection, motion tracking, positional and technical analysis
- stance and guard analysis
- hip, shoulder, and foot position tracking
- sequencing and timing analysis
- sensor inputs from heavy bag systems, wearables, accelerometers, force and workload devices, and facility telemetry

This document defines these as future SHADOW capabilities. It does not implement pipelines.

## 5) Source Authority Rule

SHADOW must not invent knowledge, guess missing facts, or fabricate certainty.

If evidence is insufficient, SHADOW must respond:

I do not currently have sufficient verified evidence to answer that.

Then SHADOW creates a research requirement.

Question -> Knowledge Gap Detected -> Research Requirement Created -> Evidence Requested -> Evidence Uploaded -> Evidence Reviewed -> Knowledge Base Expanded

Doctrine target:

Unknown Today -> Known Tomorrow

through verified evidence, never speculation.

## 9) Recursive Learning Loop Doctrine

SHADOW learning doctrine:

Observation -> Decision -> Action -> Success or Failure -> Correction -> Discovery

Unlike linear loops, every node can create a new observation.

- observation can create observation
- decision can create observation
- action can create observation
- success can create observation
- failure can create observation
- correction can create observation
- discovery can create observation

This creates a knowledge web, then an experience graph, then organizational expertise.

## 10) Failure Intelligence Doctrine

Failure is data and must be retained.

Failure handling loop:

Failure Detected -> Observation Created -> Root Cause Investigated -> Lesson Identified -> Corrective Goal Created -> Progress Tracked -> Knowledge Base Updated

Failure outputs:

1. Prevention knowledge
2. Improvement goals
3. Research requirements
4. Better future decisions

The organization should not lose lessons already paid for by experience.

## 11) Success Intelligence Doctrine

Success is also data and must be studied.

Success loop:

Success Detected -> Observation Created -> Contributing Factors Identified -> Pattern Stored -> Replication Path Created -> Knowledge Base Updated

SHADOW preserves:

- what worked
- why it worked
- who it worked for
- under what conditions
- repeatability
- teachability
- scalability

## 12) Recommendation Accountability Doctrine

Recommendations must remain traceable:

Recommendation -> Accepted or Rejected -> Implemented or Not Implemented -> Outcome Observed -> Success or Failure or Partial Result -> Knowledge Updated

Purpose: learn which recommendations actually improve outcomes.

## 14) Source Confidence Model

Source examples:

- verified internal record
- coach-verified observation

- parent report
- uploaded document

## 20) Gaps Before Azure Schema Hardens

This list was written 2026-07-19, before the multi-org migration and most of the
SHADOW runtime work. Statuses below were verified against the schema and code on
`main` on 2026-07-29; the original wording of each item is preserved so it can be
matched against older documents that cite it by number.

1. **Add canonical SHADOW event vocabulary mapping and begin emitting canonical
   event names.** — DONE for `pilot.audit_events`. The vocabulary now lives in one
   place, `apps/web/src/server/pilot/auditEventTypes.ts`, with the TypeScript type
   derived from it and `auditEventVocabulary.test.ts` asserting the SQL check
   constraint agrees. This was not merely missing: the two declarations had already
   drifted, and `shadow_research_upload_requirement` was rejected by the database
   on every SHADOW research-requirement upload.
   STILL OPEN for `pilot.shadow_events.event_name`, which is unconstrained `text`
   and is synthesized at runtime (`SHADOW_AUDIT_${TYPE}_${ENTITY}` in `audit.ts`),
   so the set of emitted names is unbounded and cannot be enumerated.
2. **Add centralized SHADOW authority-check abstraction for domain writes.** —
   PARTIAL. `shadowAuthority.ts` exists but only four modules import it, so it is
   not yet the single chokepoint this item describes.
3. **Add dedicated SHADOW telemetry writer and table.** — DONE.
   `pilot.shadow_telemetry_events` plus `shadowTelemetry.ts`.
4. **Add explicit SHADOW authority-check and promotion records as first-class
   objects.** — DONE. `pilot.shadow_authority_checks`.
5. **Add source confidence and verification-state fields for evidence-bearing
   objects.** — PARTIAL. `confidence` exists on formula facts, and
   `verification_state` on `shadow_recommendation_effectiveness` and
   `shadow_learning_events`, but per-table rather than as a general property of
   evidence-bearing objects.
6. **Reserve gym_id and program_id in SHADOW spine tables with nullable rollout.**
   — SUPERSEDED, do not implement as written. The multi-org migration landed
   2026-07-20, one day after this list, and `organization_id` IS the gym
   identifier: `/admin/organizations` creates an organization from `gymId`/
   `gymName`, and the People console labels it "Gym". It was implemented better
   than proposed here — `not null` with a foreign key, rather than a nullable
   reservation. Adding a separate `gym_id` now would create two columns meaning
   the same thing. `program_id` remains unbuilt, but there is no program entity to
   reference: "program" appears only in prose and prompts, and the nearest real
   concept is `pilot.admin_track_assignments`. Reserving a key to a table that does
   not exist would be speculative.
7. **Add research requirement workflow for knowledge gaps.** — DONE.
   `pilot.shadow_research_requirements`.
8. **Add recommendation accountability schema and event lifecycle.** — DONE.
   `pilot.shadow_recommendation_effectiveness` carries `outcome`,
   `effectiveness_score`, and a `verification_state` lifecycle
   (`unverified` → `durable_client` → `human_reviewed`).
9. **Add failure intelligence capture path tied to corrective goals and lessons.**
   — MOSTLY DONE. `pilot.shadow_learning_events` captures outcome signal,
   effectiveness, and `actions_taken`, linked to `shadow_feedback` and
   `shadow_events`. The "tied to corrective goals" half is not wired: no column
   references `pilot.goals`.

Net: items 3, 4, 7, and 8 are complete; 1 is complete for audit events and open for
shadow events; 2, 5, and 9 are partial; 6 should be struck rather than built.
