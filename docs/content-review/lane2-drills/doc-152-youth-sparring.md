How to read it:
- **INFERRED**: built from the quoted passage(s); the note says how it was adapted, and "my addition" marks anything no source says.
- **UNVERIFIED**: drafted with no source passage, from the drill's name, purpose and cues.
- Stop rules are defaults a coach can override (Backbone RULES section 1); tier CONVENTION (owner system) or UNTIERED (Long Road or unsourced).
- Scale levels: A = easier, B = the drill as designed and the starting point, C = harder, same lesson. Contact at every level = the drill's contact level.
- Contact levels are unchanged. Text already in the drill is shown under "Already in the drill" and is not changed.
- Outside research is cited by its key only, never quoted (OD-2026-10-02-013 2A); your own material is quoted.

**Difficulty rule** (no source states a difficulty for any drafted drill, so each is drafted by this rule and flagged; Jason can change the rule at any time): difficulty is what a drill needs before it can be tried at all, not its scale level. Start from the youngest or earliest stage the drill is linked to: Y1, Y2, FT1 or EX1 = beginner; Y3, T1 or RC1 = intermediate; Y4, Y5, T2 or AM1 to AM3 = advanced; pro stages only = elite. A controlled-sparring drill is at least intermediate and an open-sparring drill at least advanced. Where the drill's own text says what it needs, the draft moves one step and its note says why.

The injury, dizziness and distress stop line (DOC-027 Stop Rule Standard) is not copied into any drill: the gym-wide universal stop rules already apply to every drill (R3), so a copy would add nothing.

## D152-1. 3-Punch Limit Drill

`drl_d3d85f78276536`. Contact: controlled_sparring (unchanged). Stages: Y3, Y4, Y5. Source: DOC-152: Punxsy_Prominence_USA_Boxing_Coach_Manual_FULL.docx (Library Intake/R04 - Coaching and Coach Development); 'Youth Sparring Drill Manual', drill 8 '8. 3-Punch Limit Drill'.

**Flags:** [sparring_stop_rules] 4 drafted stop rule(s) on a controlled_sparring drill. [youth_contact] Youth-stage drill with partner contact: its stop rules are drafts for review. [difficulty_no_source] No source states a difficulty; drafted "intermediate" by the difficulty rule.

**Already in the drill, kept as written:**

- Purpose: Athletes may throw no more than three punches per exchange.
- Setup: Use pads, partner gloves, or controlled live partner work. Set a hard cap of three punches. The last punch is the structural finish and must be followed immediately by reposition.
- Steps: Maximum of three punches per combination. / Reset after each exchange. / Continue round under control.
- What good looks like: Punch count is disciplined. / The third shot looks intentional rather than accidental. / Exit begins the moment the sequence is over. / Athlete resists the urge to add a fourth punch.
- Category: tactical
- What bad looks like: Athlete adds a fourth or fifth punch because the moment feels open. / Third punch loses quality because the athlete is already leaning forward. / Exit becomes an afterthought instead of a rule. / The drill turns into flurrying instead of capped scoring.
- Cues: Quality over quantity. / Maintain stance. / Avoid overreaching.

**Target behaviour** (INFERRED)

> Caps every exchange at three punches, makes the third punch a visible, controlled finish, and repositions or resets the moment the sequence ends.

- Sources:
  - DOC-152 (Youth Sparring Drill Manual, 8. 3-Punch Limit Drill): "Maximum of three punches per combination."
  - DOC-152 (Youth Sparring Drill Manual, 8. 3-Punch Limit Drill): "Reset after each exchange."
  - DOC-027 (Punxsy Master Source Manual v1, Three and Out): "The third shot finishes the scoring phase visibly."
  - DOC-027 (Punxsy Master Source Manual v1, Three and Out): "The last punch is the structural finish and must be followed immediately by reposition."
- Note: adapted: DOC-152's cap and reset with DOC-027 Three and Out's finish and reposition lines (both kept, as D-PK88-2).
