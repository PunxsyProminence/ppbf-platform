Proportion

Every measurement in the platform descends from the golden ratio. Type climbs by √φ, space and radius follow Fibonacci, layouts split at the golden section, and the gauge bezel is a true golden rectangle. Nothing is sized by eye — which means no argument about padding ever has to be settled by taste.

Law 8 — Proportion descends from φ. Nothing is sized by eye. φ = 1.618. Its inverse is 0.618, and its square is 2.618 — the only ratio where a/b = (a+b)/a. That self-similarity is why a φ system stays coherent as it scales: a card built from these numbers has the same internal proportions as the page that holds it.

Why √φ and not φ. A full 1.618 jump between adjacent steps is too coarse for interface text — it skips straight past the sizes you actually need, so you end up inventing one and the system is broken. Two √φ steps make one φ step, so every major interval still lands exactly on the ratio while the intermediate sizes stay available.

Each term is the sum of the two before it, and consecutive ratios converge on φ — 21/13 = 1.615, 89/55 = 1.618. Radius uses the same sequence: 5 · 8 · 13 · 21.

--tap is 55px. Fibonacci, and comfortably clear of the 44px WCAG floor. The kiosk minimum type size, --t-md at 19.1px, is the √φ step. The proportion system and the accessibility floor agree — which is precisely why 55 was chosen over a rounder 56.

The golden spiral

Drawn from 377 · 233 · 144 · 89 · 55 · 34 · 21 — the same values as the space scale. A 610 × 377 rectangle, subdivided into squares, spirals inward forever at the same ratio.

The gauge bezel is 144 × 89 — consecutive Fibonacci numbers, so it is a true golden rectangle, and its needle is 55px. Colour chips are 89 × 55. The three sizes nest, which is why a metrics bar of gauges and a ramp of swatches sit on the same page without either looking borrowed.
