Training Card

The platform had no progression artefact at all: an athlete checked in, chose an effort rating, submitted, and left. Nothing accumulated, so there was nothing to come back to. A gym's real engagement mechanic is physical and visible — a card that fills up — and every primitive needed for it already existed here (.pap, .stamp, the seal), unused for this.

It is a record, not a score. Every impression corresponds to one row from /api/pilot/sessions/list, so the card cannot flatter anyone and cannot be gamed. A booked-but-not-completed session shows as an empty box, because that is the truth.

Why there is no streak

⚠
A streak would be a safety hazard here
Streaks are the obvious mechanic and the wrong one for this platform. PPBF runs a Layer 11 safety gate that locks athletes out for medical reasons — concussion protocol, injury, illness. A streak would mean an athlete watching their run die because a doctor protected them. That is a direct incentive to under-report a head injury, in a contact sport, to children.

Cumulative counts never reset and never pressure. “142 sessions logged” is just as motivating and cannot be broken by being careful.

What the laws forced

Law 2 — effort is ink, never hue. The obvious move is a red stamp for a hard session and a green one for a light one. That would put saturated colour next to the safety ladder and make “trained hard” read as “restricted”. Effort is carried by ink density instead, driven by a --ink custom property, so a heavy session looks pressed harder into the paper and no pixel lies about safety.

Law 3 — never colour alone. Each impression prints its own effort number, and carries its full date and effort as text in the accessibility tree. The card is not a wall of shades to a screen reader, and it survives greyscale printing for a board packet.

Law 8 — milestones on the Fibonacci series, like every other measure in the system: 5 · 13 · 34 · 89 · 233. Five is reachable in a fortnight so the card rewards early; the gaps then widen the way real progress does, instead of handing out a badge a week forever. An unearned seal is drawn in grey rather than hidden, so the next one is always visible.

Naming. The unearned-milestone class is .tcard-seal-slot--unearned, not --locked. In this platform “locked” is the Layer 11 safety state; borrowing that word for a gamification state is exactly the drift Law 2 exists to prevent. A milestone you have not reached is unearned. An athlete under medical hold is locked. They are not the same thing and must never share a word.
