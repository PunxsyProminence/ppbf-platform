PPBF Design System · Foundations

Materials

Five materials and one set of hardware. Everything in the platform is made of these and nothing else — that fixed vocabulary is what separates a skeuomorphic system from pastiche. All of it is generated in CSS: no image assets, so the floor kiosk renders offline.

Law 6 — Every panel is a real object. Leather, brass, slate, cork, paper. If a surface isn't one of those five, it doesn't ship. Nothing gets invented per-screen.

Leather — the ground

Hide.mat-leather

The page ground and standard panel face. Oiled espresso with a warm top-light and a heavy bottom-right falloff, so panels sit in a room rather than on a plane.

Raised hide.mat-leather--raised

Anything that lifts off the ground — tiles, tags, cards. Lighter grain, an inset bone highlight along the top edge, and a real cast shadow.

Saddle stitched.mat-stitch

A dashed bone rule inset 6px. Reserved for objects a person would physically handle: capability tiles, role tags, the buckets.

Brass — the chassis

Cast brass.mat-brass

A seven-stop gradient at 158°, not a flat gold. The specular band at 30% is what reads as metal; without it this is just a yellow rectangle.

Riveted frame.frame + .rivet

Wraps any panel. The inner well carries its own inset shadow so the contents read as sunk beneath the metal.

Grit — golden-era, poor man's gym

This is not a boutique leather shop. It's a gym that's been running on donations since the '50s: hardware oxidizes, hide cracks, and every so often something in a boxing gym gets a stain nobody talks about. This is atmosphere layered on the same five materials — not a sixth one, and never a stand-in for the safety ladder.

Worn leather.mat-leather--worn

Two crossing crack streaks multiply-blended over the raised base. Darker, flatter, less rich than the showroom hide — for the kiosk shell, the coach desk, anything that's been handled for decades.

Patina brass.frame--patina · .rivet--patina

Oxidized bronze-olive instead of gleaming gold. Reserve bright .mat-brass for the crest and a belt — everything load-bearing here hasn't seen a polishing rag in decades.

Frayed rope.rope--frayed

Same repeating gradient as .rope, patina toned down and a jagged clip-path silhouette instead of a clean rounded rectangle.

Gym Rules

No excuses. No shortcuts. Wrap your hands. Respect the ring. Anything else, take it up with the Chair.

The stain is decoration, not data. .stain uses --rust, a hue and saturation deliberately far from --locked — dried-out, brown, nowhere near the safety ladder's vivid red. It appears at most once per screen, in a fixed corner, away from any control or badge. .t-painted is the plywood-sign voice: a slight rotation and a rust-tinted second shadow standing in for hand lettering, without masking a single glyph — Law 3 still holds.

Two grounds — leather vs. warm canvas

Everything above is tuned against ink. Wrap a component in .on-canvas before using it on --canvas-warm — Guardian and Public Onboarding surfaces — or badges mix toward black and vanish into the paper.
