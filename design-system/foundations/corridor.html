Corridor & Card Catalog

Law 6 made every screen a room. Nothing let you walk between them: the platform has 63 routes and the global header linked to two of them, so the other 61 were reachable only by typing a URL. These are the two objects that fix that — a hallway, and a drawer of index cards.

Every surface sits in a room, every room opens off the corridor, and ⌘K jumps straight to any of them by name, keyword or abbreviation.

1 · The corridor

A hallway does two jobs at once, and the second is the one breadcrumbs do badly: it moves you, and it says where you are standing. Each room block wears its own .room--* ground, so the corridor reads as a row of doorways you can see through rather than a list of links. The room you are in is marked, and so is the surface.

2 · The card catalog

The file room is walled in cork and lit by a gooseneck; a drawer of index cards is the object that belongs in it. ⌘K from anywhere, or a bare / when you are not in a field — that guard matters, because a coach typing a session note must never have the slash swallowed by a palette. Arrows move, ↵ opens, esc closes and hands focus back where it came from, which is the part most palettes skip.

3 · The one rule that matters

Role filtering here is a courtesy, never a boundary. The corridor hides a door so it does not offer you something that will bounce you, and the catalog hides a card so it does not leak the shape of surfaces you cannot open. Neither is authorization. The page guard (RoleSessionGate, BoardRoleGate) and the API's own access checks remain the authority, and if this map and a guard ever disagree, the guard wins and the fix belongs in the map. Hiding a row protects nothing — never move a permission decision into navigation.
