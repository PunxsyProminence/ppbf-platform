Motion & Sound

Two systems the design system was missing. Motion existed as nine durations picked by eye (.12s, .28s, .34s, .42s, .6s…) with no relationship to each other or to anything else — the one axis that escaped Law 8. Sound did not exist at all. Both are now tokens, and both die instantly under prefers-reduced-motion.

1 · Duration — Law 8 applied to time

Fibonacci milliseconds, the same series that governs space. A drawer opening and a 21px gutter now descend from one source. Press Run to feel the lengths against each other — the numbers only mean something side by side.

2 · Easing — named for the physics

Nothing in this building floats. A stamp is driven down by a hand and stops dead against the paper; a drawer drags on its runners; a hung sign swings past level before it settles. ease describes none of that, which is why every skeuomorphic surface that uses it feels like software. Same crate, same 377ms, five curves.

--e-stampdriven and stopped — no overshoot, ever
--e-settlemass arriving under friction
--e-drawerrunners and grit — reluctant, then steady
--e-levermechanical linkage, snap at the end
--e-swingpendulum — the one curve allowed past 1

A stamp does not bounce off paper and a latch does not wobble shut, so --e-swing is the only curve here whose output passes 1. Reach for it on things that hang, and nothing else.

3 · “Working on it”

The system could say empty and done but had no word for in progress, so a submitted form looked identical to an ignored one — and the honest user response to that is to press the button again. Every pending state below carries a text cue alongside the motion, because Law 3 does not stop applying just because the channel is animation: a bare spinner is colour-and-motion-only, which is precisely what Law 3 bans.

4 · The brass focus ring

--focus existed but only buttons and inputs used it, so tabbing through a room fell through to the browser default on tags, tiles, seals and every link — a thin blue halo that belongs to no room in this building. One :focus-visible rule now covers anything focusable, and because it is :focus-visible rather than :focus, a mouse click never draws it. Press Tab to walk the row.

5 · Sound — synthesized, opt-in

Zero assets, same as everything else. There is no .mp3 or .wav in this repository: every sound below is built from oscillators and filtered noise through the Web Audio API. The kiosk renders on a cold tablet with no network, and that promise does not get an exception for audio. The bell uses inharmonic partials — 1 : 2.01 : 3.04 : 4.22 : 5.78 — because that is what separates struck bronze from a sine beep.

Off — default

Five rules, stricter than the visual system's

  1. Off by default. Always opt-in. Browsers require a user gesture before audio starts, so a page that wants sound has to ask for it.
  2. Never the only channel. Law 3 does not stop applying because the channel is audio. Every sound accompanies a visible change. A user with the volume down, on a loud floor, or with a hearing impairment loses nothing. Sound is confirmation, never information.
  3. State changes only. A check-in accepted, a lock engaged, a round bell. Never hover, never focus, never keystrokes — UI chrome that clicks is how a tool starts feeling like a toy, and this one holds medical clearances.
  4. The floor is loud. A gym floor at session time drowns a tablet speaker, so floor kiosks should generally leave this off. Sound earns its place on quiet surfaces: the office, the clinic, an after-hours kiosk.
  5. One at a time, and short. Nothing runs past ~1.2s, and an overlapping call is dropped rather than stacked. A queue of chimes is noise.

Every animation on this page is disabled entirely under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce by a single global rule, and the sound system stays silent until explicitly switched on.
