PPBF Design System · Components

Forms

Every input and select already live in components/instruments.html, but a single field only carries a coach through one line of one form. Waivers, consent, and PIN check-in are multi-field flows with their own small vocabulary on top of that — a checkbox that carries a real acknowledgment, a grid of digit wells sized for the gym floor, and a signature line that has to hold up as well on paper as it does on glass. This sheet is that vocabulary.

Checkbox

A new primitive — none exists yet in ppbf.css. Kept deliberately plain: a native square input with accent-color: var(--brass-600) for the check fill, 22px square with a 13px gap to its label so the whole row is one click target, not just the box. Focus uses the same var(--focus) brass ring as every other control in the system — no bespoke focus treatment, because Law 6 doesn't leave room for one.

PIN Entry Grid

6-Digit Floor PIN

Six individual wells rather than one text field, so an athlete can see how many digits are left at a glance under gym-floor lighting. Each box is a full --tap (55px) square set in --t-md (19.1px) mono type — Law 5's kiosk minimums — and reuses .input for the recessed leather well and the brass var(--focus) ring on focus, so nothing about the focus state had to be invented twice.

Signature Line

X
Signature

An "X" mark and a bare rule — the whole block is two elements and a mono caption, nothing more. No signature-pad canvas, no drawn-ink flourish: the system already spends its one hand-lettered voice on .t-hand for cork notes, and a signature line doesn't need a second. The rule sits at 35% brass so it reads as a line pressed into the leather rather than a form-builder's blue underline.

Contact-Risk Acknowledgment

Sparring & Contact Waiver

Boxing is a contact sport. Training and sparring carry real risk of injury, including cuts, bruising, and — rarely — more serious injury. Headgear and gloves are required for all sparring, and every session is stopped at the first sign of an athlete in distress, but no protective equipment removes the risk entirely.

The one place in the system where .stain is allowed near real content instead of staying purely atmospheric (see the README's Grit section and Law 2's --rust-* amendment). Everywhere else it sits in an empty decorative corner, away from anything a person reads or presses. Here the copy is naming an actual, literal risk of blood, so one stain is sanctioned closer to the words that describe it — while still parked in the top-right corner, away from the copy below it, still pointer-events: none, and never touching the checkbox or its label further down — the one input this panel contains. It appears exactly once on this page, which is the limit everywhere else too.
