The space itself is the first thing you earn. A brand-new athlete does not open a grid of forty grey slots — that is an inventory of everything they have not done yet, on day one. They get a hook and a blank card. The board arrives when there is something to pin to it, and the frames, mats and backings arrive after that. The room grows with the athlete, so it is never empty and never a shortfall.

The moment it is earned

A stamp coming down on paper, and nothing else — the same press the training card already uses. No bounce, no spin, no confetti, no fanfare sting. An object earned over months does not deserve a slot-machine, and a gym that celebrated like one would be telling an eleven-year-old that the noise is the point.

Backings and frames

What an athlete customises is the object they own — the board's backing and the frame around their card. Not the room: grounds carry meaning here, and an athlete picking a room would be picking a meaning.

Rarity is material, never colour. A gaming platform would rank these blue, purple, orange. Law 2 reserves saturated colour for safety and status alone, so a cosmetic tier may never borrow it — a gold badge that outranks a green one would put a decoration in the same visual register as a medical hold. Standing is carried by what a thing is made of instead: paper, then cork, then canvas, then brass. Which is also how it works in a real gym.

Not in this screen, deliberately: no leaderboard and no other athlete's card — the card is visible to the athlete, their coach and their guardian, and to nobody else. No comparison, no ranking, no "you are 4th". Everyone with access sees the same card, so there is no second version of an athlete's own record to reconcile. And no points balance anywhere: every object here came from one discrete, permanent, named accomplishment, and states which one on its face.
